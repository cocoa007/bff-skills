# HODLMM Bin Thermocouple Analyzer

## What it does

Models thermoelectric conversion dynamics across HODLMM bins. In thermoelectric physics, the Seebeck effect generates an electromotive force (EMF) when a temperature difference exists across a junction of dissimilar conductors. The voltage produced is proportional to the temperature gradient through the Seebeck coefficient: V = S * deltaT. The efficiency of thermoelectric devices is characterized by the dimensionless figure of merit ZT = S^2 * sigma * T / kappa.

In DLMM pools, each bin boundary acts as a thermocouple junction. Activity differentials between adjacent bins create "temperature gradients" — hot bins with intense trading paired with cold bins in equilibrium. The Seebeck effect converts these gradients into fee yield (EMF). Bins with high ZT are optimal thermoelectric materials — they maintain large temperature gradients while efficiently converting them to extractable EMF. Bins with low ZT are thermal short circuits — heat flows freely across junctions without generating useful voltage.

## Why agents need it

LP agents need thermocouple analysis because it reveals whether fee yield is driven by genuine activity gradients or is merely uniform background noise. A pool in THERMAL_EQUILIBRIUM has no significant temperature differences between bins — all bins are equally active or equally idle, producing no thermoelectric driving force. Only pools with sharp thermal gradients (hot active bins next to cold idle bins) generate strong Seebeck EMF.

The figure of merit ZT is the definitive thermoelectric quality metric. A junction can have a good Seebeck coefficient but if thermal conductivity is high, heat flows through the junction equalizing temperatures before the Seebeck effect can extract useful EMF. High ZT requires simultaneously: high Seebeck (strong voltage per degree), high electrical conductivity (low internal resistance), and low thermal conductivity (maintained gradient).

Thermal conductivity reveals parasitic heat flow that destroys the driving gradient. Bins in pools with high thermal conductivity act as thermal short circuits — activity spreads uniformly across the bin lattice without creating the sharp gradients needed for thermoelectric conversion.

Contact resistance identifies interface quality at bin boundaries. High contact resistance introduces parasitic Joule heating at junctions, degrading net conversion efficiency. Clean interfaces with low contact resistance enable efficient thermoelectric coupling.

Carnot efficiency bounds the theoretical maximum — no thermoelectric device can exceed the Carnot limit regardless of material quality. Pools with small temperature gradients have fundamentally low Carnot limits, constraining thermoelectric extraction even with perfect ZT materials.

## Safety notes

Read-only analysis. No transactions submitted. Only calls public Hiro and Bitflow APIs. No wallet access required. All data derived from on-chain state.

## Known constraints

- The Seebeck coefficient is approximated from reserve-weighted activity differentials between neighboring bins. Real thermoelectric materials have Seebeck coefficients determined by their electronic band structure — the DLMM analogy captures the voltage-per-temperature-difference relationship but not the underlying solid-state physics.
- Thermal conductivity in the model uses reserve distribution uniformity as a proxy. Real thermal conductivity has both electronic and phononic contributions — the DLMM model captures the net heat flow tendency but not the dual transport mechanism.
- Figure of merit ZT is computed from snapshot data. Real ZT is temperature-dependent and varies across the operating range — the snapshot provides a point estimate but not the full ZT(T) curve.
- The Carnot efficiency calculation assumes steady-state temperature differentials. Real DLMM bins experience transient, fluctuating activity that creates time-varying temperature gradients not captured by the steady-state approximation.
- Thomson coefficient estimation requires the temperature derivative of the Seebeck coefficient, which would need historical data across multiple activity levels. The snapshot approximation uses spatial variation as a proxy for temporal variation.
- Read-only; does not reflect pending mempool transactions.
- Bitflow BFF API and Hiro API must be reachable; rate limits may slow batch bin fetching.
