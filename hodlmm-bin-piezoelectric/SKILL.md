# HODLMM Bin Piezoelectric Analyzer

## What it does

Models piezoelectric transduction dynamics across HODLMM bins. In solid-state physics, the piezoelectric effect is the generation of electrical charge in response to applied mechanical stress. Certain crystals with non-centrosymmetric structure develop an electric polarization proportional to the applied stress — the direct piezoelectric effect. The converse effect applies: an applied electric field produces mechanical strain.

In DLMM pools, each bin acts as a piezoelectric crystal element. Trade pressure acts as mechanical stress applied to the crystalline reserve structure, and fee yield acts as electrical charge generated through the direct piezoelectric effect. The piezoelectric coefficient d33 = charge/stress measures how efficiently a bin converts trade pressure to fee yield. Bins with high d33 are efficient transducers — they harvest trade energy effectively. Bins with low d33 are inert crystals — they absorb stress but generate negligible charge.

## Why agents need it

LP agents need piezoelectric analysis because it reveals the stress-to-yield transduction efficiency of each bin — not just how much trade pressure it experiences, but how much of that pressure converts to extractable fee yield.

The piezoelectric coefficient is the core metric. A bin can have high stress tensor (lots of trades) but low d33 (poor charge generation) — meaning trade energy is wasted as heat rather than converted to charge. Conversely, a bin with moderate stress but high d33 generates more fee yield per unit of trade exposure.

The coupling factor reveals bidirectional energy conversion efficiency. High coupling means the bin operates as a true electromechanical transducer — stress efficiently becomes charge and charge could efficiently become strain. Low coupling means a lossy element where most energy remains in the mechanical domain.

Crystal symmetry determines whether piezoelectric response is even possible. Centrosymmetric reserve distributions cannot exhibit the piezoelectric effect regardless of applied stress — the symmetry cancels all charge generation. Only bins with broken inversion symmetry (directional reserve bias) can be piezoelectric.

Hysteresis loss reveals how much energy is wasted in each stress-strain cycle. Low hysteresis means clean, reversible transduction. High hysteresis means lossy cycling where significant trade energy dissipates as internal friction before it can generate charge.

The Curie temperature identifies how robust the crystal structure is. Bins with low Curie temperature lose their piezoelectric properties under moderate stress — the ordered crystal structure breaks down into a disordered phase. High Curie temperature bins maintain transduction under extreme conditions.

Mechanical quality factor determines whether the bin can amplify transduction through resonance. High-Q bins convert stress to charge at sharply enhanced rates near their resonant frequency. Low-Q bins provide broadband but less efficient conversion.

## Safety notes

Read-only analysis. No transactions submitted. Only calls public Hiro and Bitflow APIs. No wallet access required. All data derived from on-chain state.

Read-only check of available HODLMM pools meeting TVL threshold.
```bash
bun run hodlmm-bin-piezoelectric/hodlmm-bin-piezoelectric.ts status
```

### run
Analyzes bin piezoelectric dynamics for top pools (or a specific pool). Outputs JSON to stdout.
```bash
bun run hodlmm-bin-piezoelectric/hodlmm-bin-piezoelectric.ts run
bun run hodlmm-bin-piezoelectric/hodlmm-bin-piezoelectric.ts run --pool 1
bun run hodlmm-bin-piezoelectric/hodlmm-bin-piezoelectric.ts run --top 10
```

## Known constraints

- The constitutive piezoelectric equations assume linear response. Real DLMM bins exhibit nonlinear stress-charge relationships at high trade volumes, and the piezoelectric coefficients are approximations based on reserve-weighted fee ratios rather than true tensor components.
- Crystal symmetry analysis uses local reserve distribution variance as a proxy for lattice order. Real piezoelectric materials have specific crystallographic point groups — the DLMM analogy captures the essential requirement of broken inversion symmetry but not the full 32-point-group classification.
- Curie temperature in the model derives from current snapshot state. Real phase transitions are temperature-driven and history-dependent — the snapshot approximation identifies the current structural robustness but not the full phase diagram.
- Hysteresis loss is estimated from current efficiency gaps. Real hysteresis requires tracking the full stress-strain loop over multiple cycles, which would need historical block-by-block analysis not available in single-snapshot mode.
- Mechanical quality factor assumes steady-state resonance. Real bins experience transient, broadband stress excitation from irregular trade flow, not the single-frequency driving that Q factor traditionally characterizes.
- Read-only; does not reflect pending mempool transactions.
- Bitflow BFF API and Hiro API must be reachable; rate limits may slow batch bin fetching.
