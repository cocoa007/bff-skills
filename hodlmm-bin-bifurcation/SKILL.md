# HODLMM Bin Bifurcation Analyzer

## What it does

Analyzes critical thresholds in HODLMM bin reserve distributions where pool behavior undergoes qualitative regime changes, using concepts from dynamical systems bifurcation theory. In nonlinear dynamics, a bifurcation is a point where a small smooth change in a system parameter causes a sudden qualitative change in behavior — a phase transition. Applied to on-chain DLMM liquidity, bins approaching critical reserve thresholds can undergo abrupt transitions from populated to depleted, from symmetric to asymmetric, or from stable to oscillatory.

For each pool, the analyzer computes: saddle-node bifurcation detection identifying bins at the critical boundary between populated and depleted states (the fold catastrophe — where the stable and unstable fixed points collide and annihilate, causing reserves to collapse discontinuously), pitchfork bifurcation measuring symmetry breaking where initially balanced X/Y reserves diverge asymmetrically past a critical concentration ratio (supercritical pitchfork = gradual divergence, subcritical = explosive), Hopf bifurcation detecting transitions from stable equilibrium to oscillatory dynamics by analyzing whether the reserve gradient field exhibits growing periodic structure (limit cycle emergence), critical slowing down measuring the canonical early warning signal of approaching bifurcation — as a system nears a tipping point, it recovers more slowly from perturbations, producing rising variance and increasing lag-1 autocorrelation in the reserve field, basin of attraction estimating the stability region radius around the current equilibrium state by measuring the distance to the nearest bifurcation boundary (larger basins = more robust pools), bifurcation parameter identifying whether concentration ratio or reserve asymmetry is the dominant control variable and computing its distance to the nearest critical value, catastrophe surface mapping the fold structure of the reserve landscape to classify the bifurcation topology (fold = simple threshold, cusp = two control parameters, swallowtail = three, butterfly = four), hysteresis index quantifying the irreversibility gap at bifurcation boundaries (how far past a threshold you must reverse before the original state recovers), codimension counting the minimum number of independent parameters that must change simultaneously for the bifurcation to occur (higher codimension = more stable against single-parameter perturbations), and a composite bifurcation index scoring overall proximity to critical regime change from 0 (far from any bifurcation, deeply stable) to 100 (at or past a critical threshold, regime change imminent).

## Why agents need it

Bifurcation analysis provides the deepest possible warning of structural instability in liquidity pools. Linear metrics measure current state; bifurcation analysis measures proximity to catastrophic state changes. A pool may appear healthy by every standard metric while sitting one small perturbation away from a saddle-node collapse — the reserves look fine until they suddenly don't. Critical slowing down is the gold standard early warning signal in complex systems science, from ecosystem collapse to financial crises. Agents using bifurcation analysis can detect approaching tipping points 5-10 bins of drift before they trigger, enabling preemptive position adjustments rather than reactive scrambling. The hysteresis index reveals which transitions are irreversible — critical for understanding whether a pool that has crossed a threshold can recover naturally or requires active intervention.
