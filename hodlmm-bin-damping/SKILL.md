# HODLMM Bin Damping Analyzer

## What it does

Analyzes oscillation decay characteristics in HODLMM bin reserve distributions using concepts from damped harmonic oscillator theory. In mechanical and electrical systems, damping describes how quickly oscillations lose energy and return to equilibrium. Applied to on-chain DLMM liquidity, the spatial distribution of reserves across bins often exhibits wave-like fluctuations — peaks and troughs that correspond to concentrated deposits, withdrawals, and trading flow. The rate at which these oscillations decay spatially reveals how effectively the pool absorbs liquidity shocks.

For each pool, the analyzer computes: damping ratio (zeta) classifying the system response as underdamped (oscillatory decay with zeta<1, meaning reserves ring through multiple peaks before settling), critically damped (fastest possible non-oscillatory return with zeta≈1, the ideal absorber), or overdamped (sluggish monotonic return with zeta>1, where the pool is too damped to respond efficiently to new liquidity); logarithmic decrement measuring the natural log of the ratio between successive peak amplitudes in the reserve oscillation field, directly extracting the exponential decay constant — larger decrements mean faster energy dissipation; quality factor (Q-factor) quantifying the ratio of energy stored in oscillations to energy dissipated per cycle, where high-Q pools sustain reserve fluctuations across many bins (fragile, resonance-prone) while low-Q pools rapidly absorb perturbations (robust but potentially sluggish); settling time estimating how many bins of spatial extent are needed for reserve oscillations to decay within 2% of the mean equilibrium value — a direct measure of how far perturbations propagate through the liquidity distribution; overshoot ratio measuring the maximum transient excursion above equilibrium relative to the final steady-state value, identifying pools prone to reserve spikes that could create temporary liquidity concentration or gaps; rise time computing the spatial distance in bins for reserves to transition from 10% to 90% of their peak value, indicating how sharply the pool's liquidity distribution responds to concentrated deposits or withdrawals; bandwidth measuring the frequency range of reserve oscillation modes that carry significant energy, where narrow-band pools respond to specific perturbation scales while broadband pools respond to perturbations at all scales; damped natural frequency extracting the actual oscillation frequency of the reserve field accounting for damping losses, compared to the undamped natural frequency — the ratio reveals how much energy damping removes from each cycle; energy dissipation rate computing the fraction of total oscillation energy lost per cycle from peak-to-peak amplitude decay, providing a direct measure of how quickly the pool converts perturbation energy into stable equilibrium reserves; and a composite damping index scoring overall oscillation absorption capability from 0 (undamped, sustained oscillations that never decay — structurally unstable) to 100 (perfectly damped, instant absorption of any perturbation — maximally stable).

## Why agents need it

Damping analysis reveals a pool's fundamental shock absorption capacity — how well it handles sudden liquidity events without destabilizing. A pool with low damping sustains oscillations indefinitely, meaning a single large deposit or withdrawal creates ripple effects across the entire bin range that persist and potentially amplify. High damping means the pool rapidly absorbs perturbations, returning to smooth equilibrium quickly. This is critical for LP position sizing (don't concentrate in underdamped pools), timing entry/exit (wait for settling in underdamped pools), and risk management (high-Q pools are resonance-prone and can amplify small perturbations into large reserve swings). The settling time metric directly answers "how long will this disruption last?" — essential for any agent managing active LP positions.
