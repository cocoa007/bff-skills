# Agent Behavior — HODLMM Bin Wavelet

## Decision order
1. Run `doctor` first. If it fails, stop and surface the blocker.
2. Run `status` to confirm pools are available above TVL threshold.
3. Execute `run` with desired options. Parse JSON output.
4. Route on `structureClass`, `scaleRegime`, `waveletEntropy`, and `waveletIndex`.

## Interpreting output

- **structureClass = FLAT:** Minimal multi-scale structure. The reserve distribution is nearly constant or has a trivially simple shape. All detail coefficients are negligible — the approximation captures everything. Trade execution is maximally predictable. LP positions behave as a simple static allocation. There is nothing complex to model or monitor.
- **structureClass = SMOOTH:** Some structure but concentrated at few scales. A clean reserve profile with gentle variations at one or two spatial frequencies. The dominant scale reveals which zoom level captures the pool's character. LP strategies should be tuned to that characteristic scale.
- **structureClass = STRUCTURED:** Moderate multi-scale complexity with distinct features at several spatial frequencies. The pool has meaningful structure at multiple resolutions — fine-scale bin patterns coexist with broad-scale trends. LP positions need multi-resolution monitoring. Trading agents should check structure at the trade-size-appropriate scale.
- **structureClass = MULTISCALE:** Rich structure across many scales. The reserve distribution has significant features at most decomposition levels. Complex pool with many interacting structural patterns. Active management required. Simple single-scale strategies will miss important dynamics.
- **structureClass = FRACTAL:** Self-similar structure with energy at all scales. Maximum structural complexity — the pool looks equally complex at every zoom level. This indicates a mature, actively traded pool with structure generated by many interacting participants operating at different scales. Requires the most sophisticated analysis.
- **scaleRegime = NOISY:** High-frequency detail energy dominates. The reserve profile is dominated by bin-to-bin jitter — adjacent bins have uncorrelated reserve values. This noise may obscure any underlying trend. Denoising is essential before interpreting the reserve shape. LP positions face unpredictable per-bin variation.
- **scaleRegime = TRENDING:** Low-frequency energy dominates. The pool has a strong broad shape — reserves slope or curve smoothly across the range with little fine-scale variation. LP positions align with the dominant trend. Trade execution follows the macro reserve gradient predictably.
- **scaleRegime = BROADBAND:** Energy distributed uniformly across all scales. No single spatial frequency dominates. The pool has structure everywhere — fine detail, medium patterns, and broad trends are all equally prominent. Requires monitoring at all scales simultaneously.
- **scaleRegime = NARROWBAND:** Energy concentrated at one dominant scale. The pool's structural character is captured by a single spatial frequency. Focus analysis and LP strategy on that dominant scale — other scales are relatively quiet.
- **scaleRegime = MIXED:** No single pattern dominates. Some energy at multiple scales but without the uniformity of BROADBAND or the concentration of NARROWBAND. A typical pool with moderate complexity.
- **waveletEntropy high (>0.8):** Energy is spread across many scales. The pool is structurally complex at all resolutions. Cannot simplify the analysis to any single scale — must monitor the full multi-resolution spectrum. High maintenance burden.
- **waveletEntropy low (<0.3):** Energy concentrates at one or very few scales. The pool has a simple structural character that can be fully captured by analyzing at the dominant scale. Low maintenance — focus on the dominant frequency.
- **trendStrength > 0.8:** The approximation captures most of the reserve distribution. The pool has a strong clean trend with minor details. LP strategies should align with this dominant trend. Deviations from the trend are small and temporary.
- **trendStrength < 0.3:** The broad trend explains little of the reserve distribution. Most structure is in the detail coefficients at various scales. The pool lacks a dominant shape — LP positions cannot rely on a stable macro trend.
- **denoiseSnr > 20 dB:** Excellent signal-to-noise ratio. The reserve distribution has a clear structural signal with very little bin-level noise. Analysis of the reserve shape is reliable. Trade execution will follow the structural pattern predictably.
- **denoiseSnr < 5 dB:** Poor signal-to-noise ratio. Bin-level noise is comparable to the structural signal. The apparent reserve shape may be mostly noise. Trade execution is unpredictable. Apply denoising (remove level-1 details) before making structural interpretations.
- **crossScaleCorrelation > 0.7:** Features persist across scales. What appears at fine resolution also appears at coarse resolution — the structure is self-similar. Highly persistent patterns that are unlikely to change rapidly. Structural analysis at any scale gives similar conclusions.
- **crossScaleCorrelation < 0.2:** Scales are independent. Fine-scale structure has no relation to coarse-scale patterns. The pool's character depends entirely on which resolution you examine. Need separate analysis at each relevant scale.
- **waveletKurtosis > 5:** Intermittent extreme coefficients — the pool has sparse localized features (spikes, walls) scattered in an otherwise smooth profile. These features dominate the structural analysis despite being rare. Trading agents must watch for these localized discontinuities.
- **waveletKurtosis ≈ 3:** Gaussian-distributed coefficients — structure is distributed normally across the bins with no extreme localized features. The reserve profile has a consistent character without sudden surprises.
- **persistenceIndex > 0.7:** Structural features maintain sign across scales — a spike at fine scale corresponds to a bump at coarse scale. The structure is coherent and reinforces itself across resolutions. Stable pool structure.
- **persistenceIndex < 0.3:** Features change sign between scales — a peak at one scale corresponds to a trough at another. Cancellation effects between scales may produce a deceptively smooth aggregate appearance despite internal multi-scale complexity.

## Guardrails
- Never proceed past an error without explicit user confirmation.
- Never expose secrets or private keys in args or logs.
- Always surface error payloads with a suggested next action.
- Default to safe/read-only behavior when intent is ambiguous.
- Do not act on wavelet signals from pools with fewer than 5 populated bins — insufficient data for meaningful multi-scale decomposition.
- Do not assume FRACTAL means "bad pool" — fractal structure indicates complex multi-scale dynamics driven by diverse market participants. It requires more sophisticated strategies, not avoidance.
- Do not assume FLAT means "good pool" — flat structure may indicate a stagnant or abandoned pool with no active trading dynamics.
- Wavelet energy is scale-dependent and TVL-dependent. Compare energy values only across pools with similar TVL ranges. Normalize by total energy (use fractions) for cross-pool comparison.
- Denoise SNR is an approximation based on level-1 removal. A low SNR does not mean the pool is "broken" — it means the finest-scale structure is significant relative to broader patterns.

## On error
- Log the error payload from JSON output.
- Do not retry silently.
- Surface to user: "wavelet analysis failed: [error]" with guidance to check pool ID and API availability.

## On success
- Report the pool with highest wavelet index as the most structurally complex.
- Flag FLAT/SMOOTH pools as best for passive LP strategies and predictable trade execution.
- Flag MULTISCALE/FRACTAL pools as requiring active multi-resolution management.
- Highlight dominant scale for each pool — this is the spatial frequency where LP monitoring pays the most dividends.
- Show wavelet entropy for complexity assessment — high entropy pools need more attention at more scales.
- Report denoise SNR for reliability — low SNR means structural interpretations from other tools may be unreliable for this pool.
- For LP agents: prefer TRENDING or SMOOTH pools for passive strategies. BROADBAND pools require multi-scale monitoring. NOISY pools should be denoised before analysis.
- For trading agents: check dominant scale relative to trade size. If trade spans fewer bins than the dominant scale, the trade interacts with fine-scale noise rather than the structural signal. If trade spans more bins, the dominant structure pattern will affect execution.
